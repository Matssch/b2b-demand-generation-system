# Pipeline Measurement

## Demand Generation should eventually show up in pipeline

Traffic, leads and engagement are useful signals.

But the commercial question is:

> Is Marketing helping create valuable opportunities that Sales can convert?

The measurement model therefore moves from:

**Marketing activity → Qualified demand → Sales acceptance → Opportunity → Pipeline → Revenue**

This creates a hierarchy.

Channel metrics help diagnose performance.

Pipeline tells us whether the system is creating commercial value.

---

## 1. Start with clear funnel definitions

Marketing and Sales need to agree on what each stage means.

A practical B2B funnel could be:

### Target account

A company that fits the market and ICP.

### Engaged account

A target account showing meaningful interaction.

### Qualified demand

An account or lead with enough fit, intent and commercial relevance to justify further action.

### Sales accepted

Sales agrees the account deserves active follow-up.

### Opportunity

A real commercial buying process has been identified.

### Pipeline

The estimated commercial value of active opportunities.

### Customer

The opportunity is won.

The exact names can change.

The important part is that every stage has a shared definition.

---

## 2. The core pipeline funnel

The basic model becomes:

**Target accounts**

↓

**Engaged accounts**

↓

**Qualified demand**

↓

**Sales accepted**

↓

**Opportunities**

↓

**Pipeline**

↓

**Customers**

↓

**Revenue**

Each conversion tells us something different about the health of the system.

---

## 3. Target Account → Engagement

This shows whether Marketing is reaching and engaging the right market.

Example:

**1,000 target accounts**

**250 engaged accounts**

Account engagement rate:

**250 / 1,000 = 25%**

Useful breakdowns include:

- Segment
- Industry
- Campaign
- Geography
- Account tier
- Buying-group role

A low engagement rate may indicate:

- Weak targeting
- Weak message
- Poor channel choice
- Limited market relevance

---

## 4. Engagement → Qualified Demand

This tells us whether engagement is commercially useful.

Example:

**250 engaged accounts**

**75 qualified accounts**

Qualification rate:

**75 / 250 = 30%**

If engagement is high but qualification is weak, Marketing may be attracting attention from the wrong companies.

This is why engagement volume should not be treated as the final result.

---

## 5. Sales Acceptance Rate

One of the most useful Marketing/Sales alignment metrics is:

**Sales Accepted Accounts / Qualified Accounts**

Example:

**75 qualified accounts**

**60 Sales accepted**

Sales acceptance rate:

**60 / 75 = 80%**

A declining acceptance rate may indicate:

- ICP drift
- Weak scoring
- Poor campaign targeting
- Weak qualification
- Misalignment between Marketing and Sales

---

## 6. Qualified Demand → Opportunity

The next question is:

> Does qualified demand actually become pipeline?

Example:

**60 Sales accepted accounts**

**18 opportunities**

Opportunity conversion:

**18 / 60 = 30%**

This is a stronger quality signal than lead volume.

Marketing may generate fewer qualified accounts while still creating more opportunities.

That can be a better commercial outcome.

---

## 7. Pipeline Created

Once opportunities exist, their commercial value becomes important.

Example:

| Opportunity | Value |
|---|---:|
| A | €20,000 |
| B | €35,000 |
| C | €50,000 |
| D | €25,000 |

Total pipeline created:

**€130,000**

This allows Marketing performance to move beyond:

> We generated 18 opportunities.

toward:

> We generated €X of qualified pipeline.

---

## 8. Average Opportunity Value

Useful calculation:

**Pipeline Value / Opportunities**

Example:

**€360,000 pipeline**

across:

**18 opportunities**

Average opportunity value:

**€20,000**

This matters when comparing segments.

For example:

### Segment A

20 opportunities × €5K

= €100K pipeline

### Segment B

8 opportunities × €30K

= €240K pipeline

Segment A created more opportunities.

Segment B created more commercial value.

---

## 9. Cost per Opportunity

Marketing investment should also connect to pipeline creation.

Example:

**€50,000 Marketing spend**

produces:

**20 opportunities**

Cost per opportunity:

**€2,500**

This can be compared across:

- Channels
- Segments
- Campaigns
- Markets

But opportunity quality also matters.

A €2,500 opportunity cost may be excellent for a €100K deal and terrible for a €3K deal.

---

## 10. Pipeline per €1 of Marketing Investment

Another useful efficiency metric is:

**Pipeline Created / Marketing Spend**

Example:

**€500,000 pipeline**

from:

**€50,000 Marketing investment**

creates:

**€10 pipeline per €1 invested**

This is not the same as revenue ROI.

Pipeline can still be lost.

But it creates a useful intermediate measure before deals close.

---

## 11. Pipeline by Segment

The segment model should be reflected in reporting.

Example:

| Segment | Qualified | Opportunities | Pipeline | Avg. Opportunity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Small | 120 | 30 | €150K | €5K |
| Mid-market | 60 | 20 | €400K | €20K |
| Strategic | 20 | 8 | €480K | €60K |

This immediately shows why lead volume alone can be misleading.

The smallest segment generates the most volume.

The strategic segment generates the most value per opportunity.

The correct investment decision depends on:

**Volume + Efficiency + Value + Capacity**

---

## 12. Pipeline by Campaign

Campaign reporting should connect execution to commercial output.

Example:

| Campaign | Spend | Qualified | Opportunities | Pipeline |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Search Capture | €20K | 45 | 15 | €250K |
| LinkedIn Demand | €15K | 30 | 8 | €210K |
| Webinar | €8K | 20 | 7 | €180K |
| ABM Pilot | €12K | 10 | 5 | €300K |

The ABM pilot generates fewer opportunities.

But those opportunities carry much more value.

This changes how performance should be interpreted.

---

## 13. Created vs Influenced Pipeline

This distinction matters.

### Pipeline created

Marketing activity played a clear role in generating the opportunity.

### Pipeline influenced

Marketing interacted with or supported an opportunity that may have originated elsewhere.

Both can be useful.

But they should not be presented as the same thing.

Otherwise Marketing can eventually claim influence over almost every deal in the CRM.

That makes the metric impressive and mostly useless.

---

## 14. Pipeline Velocity

Pipeline value alone does not tell us how quickly commercial value is moving.

Pipeline velocity can be influenced by:

- Opportunity volume
- Average deal value
- Win rate
- Sales cycle

A simplified logic is:

**More opportunities**

×

**Higher value**

×

**Higher win rate**

÷

**Shorter sales cycle**

=

**Faster revenue creation**

Marketing can affect several of these factors.

For example:

Better education before Sales involvement may improve:

- Opportunity quality
- Buying-group engagement
- Sales cycle
- Win rate

---

## 15. Win Rate

Eventually, Marketing should understand what happens after opportunity creation.

Example:

**20 opportunities**

**5 customers**

Win rate:

**25%**

Useful breakdowns include:

- Segment
- Campaign
- Acquisition source
- ICP tier
- Industry
- Buying-group coverage

If one campaign generates a lot of opportunities but almost none close, it may not deserve more budget.

---

## 16. Pipeline Cohorts

Pipeline should also be analysed over time.

For example:

### January cohort

Accounts first qualified in January.

Track:

- Sales acceptance
- Opportunities
- Pipeline
- Wins

### February cohort

Do the same.

This avoids comparing very new opportunities with older opportunities that have had much more time to close.

Cohort analysis is especially important when the sales cycle is long.

---

## 17. Source should not be confused with causality

A CRM may show:

**Source = Paid Search**

That does not necessarily mean Paid Search created the entire buying process.

The account may previously have:

- Seen LinkedIn campaigns
- Attended an event
- Read organic content
- Spoken with Sales
- Received email nurture

I would therefore use attribution as a decision aid rather than pretend it provides perfect causal truth.

Useful views include:

- First touch
- Lead source
- Opportunity source
- Campaign influence
- Account engagement
- Sales feedback

---

## 18. Executive Demand Gen Dashboard

At leadership level, I would keep the reporting relatively simple.

### Demand

- Target accounts
- Engaged accounts
- Qualified demand

### Sales alignment

- Sales acceptance rate
- Time to follow-up

### Pipeline

- Opportunities
- Pipeline created
- Average opportunity value
- Cost per opportunity
- Pipeline / Marketing spend

### Revenue

- Wins
- Win rate
- Revenue
- CAC
- Payback where available

Everything underneath should help explain why these numbers are changing.

---

## 19. Diagnostic hierarchy

If pipeline is weak, I would diagnose backwards.

### Pipeline down

Check opportunities.

### Opportunities down

Check Sales acceptance and qualification.

### Qualification down

Check account fit and engagement.

### Engagement down

Check audience, message and channels.

### Target quality down

Revisit ICP and market selection.

The system becomes:

**Pipeline problem**

↓

**Funnel diagnosis**

↓

**Constraint**

↓

**Action**

This is more useful than simply increasing campaign volume.

---

## 20. The commercial feedback loop

The complete measurement model becomes:

**Market**

↓

**ICP**

↓

**Target accounts**

↓

**Engagement**

↓

**Qualified demand**

↓

**Sales acceptance**

↓

**Opportunity**

↓

**Pipeline**

↓

**Revenue**

↓

**Customer quality**

↓

**Learn**

↓

**Improve ICP, campaigns and investment**

This is how Demand Generation becomes a commercial operating system rather than a lead-generation function.

---

## Core principle

> **The objective of B2B Demand Generation is not maximum lead volume. It is predictable creation of valuable pipeline.**
