# Buying Group Model
## Accounts do not buy. People inside accounts do.
For larger B2B opportunities, one lead rarely represents the full buying process. Different people can influence the decision for different reasons. A useful demand generation model therefore needs to move from:

**Lead → Account → Buying group**
The goal is to understand:

- Who experiences the problem
- Who owns the budget
- Who evaluates the solution
- Who can block the decision
- Who ultimately approves the purchase
- Who will use the solution

This is especially important in larger and more complex deals.

---

## 1. Why buying groups matter
A single contact may:
- Download content
- Attend a webinar
- Request information
- Visit pricing pages
- Engage with Sales

But that does not mean they control the decision.nIn many B2B purchases, the buying process includes multiple stakeholders. For example:
- A functional lead may feel the operational pain
- A manager may evaluate the solution
- Finance may question the economics
- IT may review technical fit
- Procurement may control the commercial process
- An executive may approve the investment
- End users may determine adoption

Marketing needs to understand these roles.

---

## 2. Typical buying-group roles
A practical model could include:

### Problem owner
The person closest to the business problem. They are often the first to recognise the need. Their questions may include:
- How do we solve this problem?
- What is creating the current inefficiency?
- What are other companies doing?

---

### Champion
The person actively pushing the solution internally. A strong champion helps:

- Build internal support
- Share information
- Navigate stakeholders
- Maintain momentum

Marketing can support this person with useful proof, business cases and internal-ready content.

---

### Decision-maker
The person with authority to approve or reject the purchase. They often care about:

- Business impact
- Risk
- Strategic value
- Cost
- Expected return

Their content needs are usually different from those of an operational user.

---

### Economic buyer
The person responsible for the financial decision. This may be:
- Finance
- CFO
- Budget owner
- Business unit leader

They may require:
- ROI
- Payback
- Commercial impact
- Cost comparison
- Business case

---

### Technical evaluator
The stakeholder reviewing technical feasibility. Depending on the product, this could include:
- IT
- Security
- Data
- Engineering
- Product
- Operations

Typical questions include:
- Will it integrate?
- Is it secure?
- Is implementation realistic?
- Does it meet technical requirements?

---

### Procurement / Legal
These stakeholders often become relevant later in the buying process. They may focus on:
- Contracts
- Risk
- Compliance
- Procurement rules
- Vendor terms
- Data processing

They may not create demand, but they can slow or block an opportunity.

---

### End user
The person who will actually use or experience the solution. End users often care about:
- Ease of use
- Practical value
- Workflow impact
- Adoption
- Training

Their support can matter even when they do not own the budget.

---

## 3. Buying groups vary by segment
The buying group should not be overcomplicated for every account.

### Smaller accounts
The buyer may also be:
- User
- Budget owner
- Decision-maker

One person can perform several roles. The journey can therefore remain relatively simple.

### Mid-market
More roles start to appear. For example:
- Problem owner
- Manager
- Finance
- Decision-maker

Marketing and Sales may need to influence several people.

### Strategic accounts
The buying group becomes more complex. The account may involve:
- Business sponsor
- Champion
- Multiple users
- Finance
- IT
- Security
- Procurement
- Legal
- Executive leadership

The larger the opportunity, the more important stakeholder coverage becomes.

---

## 4. Map message to stakeholder
Different stakeholders need different reasons to care. A simple messaging model could look like:

| Stakeholder | Primary concern | Marketing message |
|---|---|---|
| Problem owner | Solve operational pain | Practical impact |
| Champion | Build internal support | Proof + business case |
| Manager | Team/business performance | Efficiency + outcomes |
| Economic buyer | Financial return | ROI + cost |
| Executive | Strategic value | Growth + risk |
| IT / Technical | Feasibility | Integration + security |
| Procurement | Commercial risk | Terms + compliance |
| End user | Usability | Experience + ease |

One generic campaign message is unlikely to work equally well for all of them.

---

## 5. Content should support the internal buying process
B2B content is not only about attracting external attention. It should also help buyers make the case internally. Examples include:

### Awareness content
Helps the problem owner understand the issue.

### Educational content
Helps stakeholders understand possible approaches.

### Case studies
Help champions prove that the solution works.

### ROI / business case
Helps the economic buyer understand financial value.

### Technical documentation
Helps technical stakeholders evaluate feasibility.

### Comparison content
Helps the buying group understand trade-offs.

### Executive summary
Helps senior decision-makers quickly understand the commercial case.

The objective is to reduce friction inside the buying organisation.

---

## 6. Buying-group engagement
For larger accounts, I would not only measure whether one lead engaged. I would also look at:
- Number of engaged contacts
- Roles engaged
- Seniority
- Engagement depth
- Content consumed
- Repeat activity
- Time between interactions
- Sales conversations

This gives us a more useful question: Is the account becoming more engaged as a buying group? Rather than: did one person download another PDF?

---

## 7. Account coverage
A simple buying-group coverage model could be:

### Low coverage
One engaged contact.

### Developing coverage
Several contacts, but mostly one function.

### Strong coverage
Multiple relevant roles engaged.

### Buying-group coverage
Champion + decision-maker + supporting stakeholders engaged. This can help Marketing and Sales identify risk. For example:

+ An opportunity may look strong because the champion is highly engaged.

But if the economic buyer has never engaged and Sales has no access to them, the deal may still be fragile.

---

## 8. Buying groups change the role of Marketing
In a lead-centric model:

**Marketing generates lead → Sales takes over**

In a buying-group model: 

**Marketing creates demand** → **Multiple stakeholders engage** → **Sales enters** → **Marketing continues influencing the account** → **Opportunity progresses**

Marketing does not automatically stop when Sales begins. For larger opportunities, both teams influence the account throughout the journey.

---

## 9. Sales feedback improves the model
Marketing cannot identify every buying-group role using digital data alone. Sales conversations provide important information. Sales can help identify:
- Actual decision-maker
- Champion
- Missing stakeholders
- Internal objections
- Procurement process
- Competitors
- Timing
- Buying risk

Marketing can then use this information to adjust:
- Messaging
- Content
- Campaigns
- Account targeting

This creates another feedback loop:

**Marketing engagement** → **Sales insight** → **Buying-group understanding**  → **Better Marketing support** → **Opportunity progression**

---

## 10. Buying-group scoring
For strategic accounts, buying-group engagement can become part of account scoring. For example:

| Signal | Example score |
|---|---:|
| Problem owner engaged | +10 |
| Multiple contacts engaged | +10 |
| Decision-maker engaged | +20 |
| Economic buyer engaged | +20 |
| Technical stakeholder engaged | +10 |
| High-intent page visit | +10 |
| Sales meeting completed | +20 |

The exact scoring model should be validated with actual win/loss data. The purpose is not to create a complicated scoring system for its own sake. The purpose is to identify accounts where real buying momentum is building.

---

## 11. Buying-group risk
I would also look for missing stakeholders. Example:

An opportunity has:
- Strong champion
- Multiple content interactions
- Sales engagement

But:
- No economic buyer
- No executive sponsor
- No technical validation

This should not automatically be considered a healthy opportunity. Buying-group coverage can reveal where the deal is vulnerable.

---

## The buying-group model

The progression becomes:

**Target account** → **Problem owner** → **Champion** → **Decision-maker** → **Economic / technical stakeholders** → **Buying-group engagement** → **Opportunity** → **Pipeline**

This is a much stronger model than measuring individual leads in isolation.

---

## Core principle
**In complex B2B sales, the unit of growth is not the lead. It is the account and the buying group inside it.**

Marketing should help the right people understand the problem, build confidence in the solution and support the internal path toward a commercial decision.
