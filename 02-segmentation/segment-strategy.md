# Segment Strategy

## Different customers require different growth motions
Once the ICP is clear, the next question is not: Which campaign should we run?
It is: Which commercial journey fits each type of customer?

A strong demand generation engine should not force every account into the same funnel. Customer value, buying complexity and Sales involvement change by segment. The operating model becomes:

**ICP → Segment → Buying motion → Channel mix → Sales involvement → Pipeline**

---

## 1. Why segmentation matters
A smaller customer may be able to:
- Understand the product quickly
- Compare options independently
- Purchase with limited Sales involvement
- Complete most of the journey digitally

A larger account may require:
- Multiple stakeholders
- More education
- Stronger proof
- Procurement
- Security or legal review
- Commercial negotiation
- Sales involvement

These are fundamentally different buying journeys. Marketing should reflect that.

---

## 2. Example segmentation model
A practical starting model could be:

| Segment | Primary motion | Marketing role | Sales role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–20 | Self-service | Acquire + convert | Limited |
| 21–50 | Hybrid | Create + qualify demand | Moderate |
| 50+ | Sales-led | Create + influence demand | High |

The exact boundaries depend on the business. The important principle is that the commercial motion changes as customer value and buying complexity increase.

---

# Segment 1 — Smaller accounts
## Typical profile
Smaller businesses with a relatively simple buying process. The economics usually require a scalable acquisition model. The objective is:

> **Acquire and convert efficiently without unnecessary Sales cost.**

### Primary motion
**Marketing-led / self-service**

Typical journey:

**Search / Paid Social / SEO** → **Landing page** → **Conversion** → **Automated onboarding** → **Customer**

---

## Marketing priorities
Marketing carries most of the journey. Focus areas include:

- Paid Search
- SEO
- Paid Social
- Conversion optimisation
- Landing pages
- Automated email
- Product education
- Retargeting
- Self-service onboarding

The experience should reduce friction. A strong customer should not need a Sales conversation simply because the internal organisation is structured that way.

---

## Key metrics
Useful metrics include:

- CAC / CPA
- Conversion rate
- Customer volume
- Cost per conversion
- Activation rate
- Payback
- Retention
- Customer value

The key constraint is usually:

**Efficiency at scale**

---

# Segment 2 — Mid-market
## Typical profile
Larger opportunities with more commercial value and slightly more complex buying needs. These accounts may still enter digitally but require human involvement before conversion. The objective becomes:

> **Use Marketing to create and qualify demand before introducing Sales at the right moment.**

### Primary motion
**Hybrid Marketing + Sales**

Typical journey:

**Demand creation** → **Website / content engagement** → **Lead or account signal** → **Qualification** → **Sales** → **Opportunity**

---

## Marketing priorities
Marketing supports both acquisition and qualification. Typical activities could include:

- Paid Search
- LinkedIn
- Retargeting
- Case studies
- Webinars
- Commercial content
- Email nurture
- Comparison content
- Solution pages
- Customer proof

The objective is not simply to generate a form submission. Marketing should help the buyer understand:
- The problem
- The solution
- The business value
- Why the company should be considered

---

## Sales involvement
Sales should become involved when there is enough evidence of:
- Fit
- Intent
- Commercial potential

Possible triggers include:
- Demo request
- High-value content engagement
- Multiple relevant page visits
- Strong ICP score
- Event attendance
- Pricing engagement
- Repeated account activity

This reduces unnecessary handoffs.

---

## Key metrics
Useful metrics include:
- Qualified leads
- Sales acceptance rate
- Lead-to-opportunity rate
- Cost per qualified lead
- Pipeline created
- Opportunity value
- Win rate
- Sales cycle

The key constraint is usually: **Lead quality and conversion into pipeline**

---

# Segment 3 — Larger / strategic accounts
## Typical profile
Larger companies with higher potential commercial value and more complex buying processes. The buying journey may involve:

- Multiple stakeholders
- Procurement
- Finance
- Technical teams
- Legal
- Executive decision-makers
- End users

The objective becomes: **Create demand inside the account and help Sales progress a complex buying process.**

### Primary motion
**Sales-led + account-based Marketing**

Typical journey:

**Target account** → **Buying group** → **Account engagement** → **Sales coordination** → **Opportunity** → **Pipeline** → **Revenue**

---

## Marketing priorities
The role of Marketing changes significantly. Instead of maximising lead volume, Marketing may focus on:

- Target-account awareness
- Buying-group engagement
- Executive content
- Case studies
- Industry-specific messaging
- Events
- Webinars
- Personalised campaigns
- Paid LinkedIn
- Account-level retargeting
- Sales enablement
- Partnerships

Marketing increasingly supports opportunity creation and progression.

---

## Sales involvement
Sales becomes central. Marketing and Sales should agree on:
- Priority accounts
- Buying groups
- Account signals
- Messaging
- Outreach timing
- Opportunity strategy
- Campaign support

The account becomes the shared unit of focus. Not the individual lead.

---

## Key metrics
Useful metrics include:
- Target-account coverage
- Engaged accounts
- Buying-group engagement
- Account penetration
- Meetings
- Opportunities
- Pipeline value
- Pipeline velocity
- Win rate
- Revenue

The key constraint is usually: **Creating and progressing valuable opportunities**

---

# The progression
The commercial motion evolves with account value and complexity.

### Smaller accounts
**Traffic → Conversion → Customer**

### Mid-market
**Demand → Qualification → Sales → Opportunity**

### Strategic accounts
**Account → Buying group → Engagement → Opportunity → Pipeline**

This prevents Marketing from using one funnel for fundamentally different customers.

---

## Channel strategy by segment
| Channel / tactic | Smaller | Mid-market | Strategic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Paid Search | High | High | Medium |
| SEO | High | High | Medium |
| Paid Social | High | High | Targeted |
| Retargeting | High | High | High |
| Email nurture | Automated | High | Personalised |
| Webinars | Low/Medium | High | High |
| Case studies | Medium | High | Very high |
| Events | Low | Medium | High |
| Sales outreach | Low | Medium | Very high |
| ABM | Low | Light | High |
| Executive content | Low | Medium | High |

The table is directional. Actual investment should be based on real performance and customer economics.

---

## Budget should follow customer economics
Segmentation should also influence acquisition cost expectations. For example:

A smaller account with limited lifetime value may require a strict CAC threshold.

A strategic account with significant revenue potential can justify:

- Higher acquisition cost
- More Sales time
- More personalised content
- Events
- Account-based advertising
- Longer nurture

This means one universal CAC target can be misleading. A better model is:

**Customer value → Acceptable acquisition investment → GTM motion**

---

## Avoiding funnel leakage
Segmentation also helps prevent common handoff problems. For example:

### Problem
Every form submission goes directly to Sales.

### Result
Sales receives:
- Low-value prospects
- Poor-fit companies
- Early-stage researchers

### Better model
Use segment and intent together.

**Small + high intent**
→ Digital conversion

**Mid-market + high intent**
→ Qualification / Sales

**Strategic + relevant engagement**
→ Account-level Sales coordination

This gives Sales a clearer reason to act.

---

## Segment strategy should evolve
The first segmentation model is a hypothesis. Over time, I would validate it against:
- CAC
- Lead quality
- Opportunity rate
- Deal size
- Win rate
- Sales cycle
- Retention
- Expansion
- Lifetime value

That creates a feedback loop:

**Segment hypothesis**  → **GTM motion** → **Pipeline** → **Customer economics** → **Segment refinement**

The goal is not to preserve the original segmentation. The goal is to learn which commercial motion actually works.

---

## Core principle
**Segmentation should change how the company goes to market, not just how the CRM is organised.**
Different customer economics require different levels of Marketing automation, Sales involvement and account-level investment.
