# B2B Campaign Architecture
## A campaign should connect to a commercial outcome
A campaign should not start with: We need a LinkedIn campaign.

It should start with: Which commercial problem are we trying to solve, for which audience, and what behaviour do we need to create? 

The campaign architecture therefore follows:

**Business objective → ICP → Segment → Problem → Buying group → Message → Channel → Experience → Qualification → Sales → Pipeline**

Channels sit inside the system. They are not the strategy.

---

## 1. Start with the commercial objective
Every campaign should have a clear job. Examples could include:
- Create awareness inside a new ICP segment
- Increase engagement from strategic accounts
- Generate qualified mid-market demand
- Capture high-intent search demand
- Support an existing opportunity
- Re-engage inactive target accounts
- Increase pipeline in a specific market

This determines how the campaign should be designed and measured. A campaign designed to create awareness should not be judged only on immediate demo requests. A campaign designed to capture existing demand should.

---

## 2. Define the target audience
The next layer is: Who exactly are we trying to influence? I would define:

### ICP
Which companies fit?

### Segment
Which commercial motion applies?

### Account tier
How much investment does the account justify?

### Buying group
Which people inside the company matter?

This prevents broad targeting from becoming the default.

---

## 3. Start messaging with the problem
The campaign message should connect to a problem the buyer recognises. A useful structure is:

**Problem** → **Business consequence** → **Better outcome** → **Solution** → **Proof** → **Next step**

For example:
Instead of: Our platform provides AI training for enterprises. A stronger starting point could be: AI adoption stalls when teams have access to tools but lack the skills and judgment to use them effectively. The product enters after the problem is understood.

---

## 4. Match the message to the buying group
Different stakeholders may need different versions of the same commercial story.

### Executive
Message:
- Strategic transformation
- Competitive advantage
- Risk
- Business impact

### Functional leader
Message:
- Capability
- Productivity
- Team performance
- Implementation

### Finance
Message:
- Commercial return
- Efficiency
- Cost
- Payback

### Technical stakeholder
Message:
- Feasibility
- Integration
- Security
- Scalability

The core problem stays consistent. The reason to care changes by stakeholder.

---

## 5. Define the campaign offer
The offer is what gives the buyer a reason to act. It should match the level of intent.

### Early-stage offers
Examples:
- Industry report
- Benchmark
- Guide
- Webinar
- Assessment
- Thought leadership

### Mid-stage offers
Examples:
- Case study
- Framework
- Workshop
- ROI model
- Buyer guide

### High-intent offers
Examples:
- Demo
- Consultation
- Assessment
- Trial
- Proposal

The mistake is asking every buyer to: Book a demo. Some buyers need more reason to care first.

---

## 6. Build the channel mix around the objective
Channels should have specific jobs. For example, a mid-market demand generation campaign could use:

### LinkedIn
Create awareness and reach relevant roles.

### Content
Explain the problem and build credibility.

### Webinar
Create deeper engagement.

### Email
Continue the journey.

### Retargeting
Stay visible to engaged buyers.

### Paid Search
Capture demand when active intent develops.

### Sales outreach
Convert qualified account engagement into conversation.

Together:

**Paid Social** → **Content** → **Website engagement** → **Retargeting / nurture** → **Intent** → **Sales**

A multi-channel campaign is not simply the same advertisement copied across five platforms. Each channel should move the buyer forward.

---

## 7. Design the landing experience
The campaign does not stop at the click. The landing page should continue the same commercial story. The sequence is:

**Ad / message** → **Landing-page headline** → **Problem** → **Value** → **Proof** → **CTA**

Message continuity matters. If the campaign discusses one specific problem and the user lands on a generic homepage, much of the relevance is lost.

---

## 8. Build the follow-up before launch
A campaign should not generate demand before the next step is ready. Before launch, I want to know:
- What happens after conversion?
- Does the lead enter CRM correctly?
- Is the account identified?
- Is enrichment required?
- Is there nurture?
- Does Sales receive a notification?
- Who owns follow-up?
- What qualifies the account?
- What happens if the account is not ready?

The complete campaign is: **Ad → Landing page → Conversion → CRM → Qualification → Follow-up**
Not: **Ad → Form submission → Marketing celebrates**

---

## 9. Campaign example — Mid-market demand generation
Imagine the commercial objective is: Generate qualified pipeline from mid-market companies experiencing an AI capability gap.

### ICP
Companies with:
- Relevant company size
- Active AI initiatives
- Clear skills gap
- Budget potential
- Strategic need for workforce adoption

### Buying group
Potential stakeholders:
- Transformation leader
- HR / L&D
- Business unit leader
- Technology leader
- Executive sponsor

### Problem
AI tools are being introduced faster than employees can build the capability required to use them effectively.

### Campaign theme
**Turn AI access into organisational capability**

### Content
- AI capability benchmark
- Enterprise AI skills guide
- Customer case
- Webinar
- Transformation framework

### Channels
- LinkedIn
- Paid Search
- Organic content
- Retargeting
- Email
- Sales outreach

### Conversion
Primary: **AI capability assessment**
Secondary: **Speak with an expert**

### Qualification
Account evaluated against:
- ICP fit
- Company size
- Engagement
- Buying signals
- Commercial value

### Sales
Strong-fit accounts showing meaningful intent enter the Sales motion.

### Measurement
- Target-account reach
- Engaged accounts
- Qualified demand
- Sales acceptance
- Opportunities
- Pipeline generated

This connects campaign execution directly to commercial performance.

---

## 10. Campaign architecture by segment
Different segments require different campaign models.

### Smaller accounts

**Paid / SEO → Landing page → Conversion → Automated lifecycle**

Optimisation focus:
- CPA
- Conversion
- Volume
- Activation

### Mid-market

**Demand creation → Content → Engagement → Qualification → Sales**

Optimisation focus:
- Qualified demand
- Sales acceptance
- Opportunity rate
- Pipeline

### Strategic accounts

**Target account → Buying group → Coordinated campaigns → Sales → Opportunity**

Optimisation focus:
- Account engagement
- Buying-group coverage
- Meetings
- Pipeline progression

The campaign architecture follows customer economics.

---

## 11. Build campaigns as hypotheses
Every campaign should contain an assumption we can test.

Example: We believe operational leaders at mid-market companies experiencing AI adoption challenges will engage more strongly with capability-gap messaging than generic AI training messaging. Then define:

### Audience

Who are we testing?

### Message
What commercial problem are we testing?

### Channel
Where will we reach them?

### Behaviour
What do we expect them to do?

### Success metric
What tells us the hypothesis has potential?

This creates: **Hypothesis → Execute → Measure → Learn → Scale / Change / Stop**

---

## 12. Separate diagnostic metrics from commercial metrics
Campaign reporting should have multiple layers.

### Media diagnostics
- Impressions
- CPM
- CTR
- CPC
- Frequency

### Engagement diagnostics
- Landing-page visits
- Content engagement
- Webinar registrations
- Repeat visits

### Demand metrics
- Leads
- Engaged accounts
- Qualified accounts
- Intent signals

### Commercial metrics
- Sales accepted
- Opportunities
- Pipeline
- Customers
- Revenue

The mistake is treating the first layer as the outcome. A 2% CTR is interesting. A €1M qualified pipeline is considerably more interesting.

---

## 13. Campaign portfolio
I would not expect every campaign to perform the same job. A healthy portfolio may contain:

### Demand creation
Build future opportunity.

### Demand capture
Convert existing intent.

### Segment campaigns
Develop specific ICP segments.

### Account campaigns
Influence strategic accounts.

### Opportunity campaigns
Help Sales progress active deals.

### Customer campaigns
Support expansion and advocacy. This prevents the Marketing calendar from becoming a collection of disconnected launches.

---

## 14. Campaign review
After a campaign, I would review:

### Market
Did we target the right segment?

### Message
Did the problem resonate?

### Channel
Where did engagement occur?

### Funnel
Where did buyers drop out?

### Qualification
Was the demand commercially useful?

### Sales
Did Sales accept and progress it?

### Pipeline
Did the campaign create or influence opportunity?

### Decision
What should we: **Scale → Improve → Test again → Stop**

The final output should be a decision. Not another campaign report.

---

## Campaign system
The complete architecture becomes:

**Commercial objective** → **ICP** → **Segment** → **Buying group** → **Problem** → **Message** → **Offer** → **Channel mix** → **Landing experience** → **CRM** → **Qualification** → **Sales** → **Pipeline** → **Learning**

This is the unit I would use to design B2B demand generation campaigns.

---

## Core principle
**A campaign is not a channel execution. It is a commercial hypothesis connected to a measurable buying journey.**
