# Sales Alignment & Handoff

## Demand generation breaks when the handoff breaks

Generating qualified demand is only useful if Sales knows:

- What happened
- Why the account matters
- Who is involved
- What the next action should be

A weak handoff turns good Marketing activity into lost pipeline.

The goal is therefore not:

> Marketing generates leads and Sales follows up.

The better model is:

**Marketing identifies demand → Sales receives context → Sales acts → Feedback returns to Marketing**

---

## 1. Agree on what "qualified" means

Marketing and Sales should define qualification together.

A useful qualification model combines:

- ICP fit
- Commercial value
- Intent
- Engagement
- Buying-group signals
- Timing

This prevents Marketing from calling every conversion qualified.

It also prevents Sales from rejecting leads without a shared standard.

---

## 2. Define the handoff trigger

The handoff should happen when there is enough evidence that Sales involvement creates value.

Possible triggers include:

### Strong explicit intent

- Demo request
- Contact request
- Pricing enquiry
- Proposal request

### Strong account behaviour

- Repeated high-intent visits
- Multiple stakeholders engaging
- Relevant event attendance
- Strong content consumption

### Strong commercial fit

- High ICP score
- Strategic account
- High expected value
- Clear problem fit

The trigger should reflect both:

**Fit + Intent**

---

## 3. Different segments need different handoffs

The handoff should follow the GTM motion.

### Smaller accounts

Marketing may own almost the full journey.

**Demand → Conversion → Automated onboarding**

Sales involvement is limited.

---

### Mid-market

Marketing qualifies the opportunity before passing it to Sales.

**Demand → Engagement → Qualification → Sales**

Sales should receive enough context to continue the conversation.

---

### Strategic accounts

The handoff is less binary.

Marketing and Sales work the account together.

**Target account → Engagement → Buying group → Sales coordination → Opportunity**

Marketing may continue influencing the account after Sales becomes involved.

---

## 4. What Sales should receive

A useful handoff contains more than:

> New lead: John Smith

Sales should ideally receive:

### Account information

- Company
- Segment
- Industry
- Geography
- ICP score
- Estimated commercial value

### Contact information

- Name
- Role
- Seniority
- Buying-group role

### Engagement

- Pages visited
- Content consumed
- Event attendance
- Email engagement
- High-intent actions
- Recent activity

### Marketing context

- Campaign
- Original source
- Message or offer
- Relevant pain point

### Qualification reason

Most importantly:

> Why is this account being passed to Sales now?

This makes the handoff actionable.

---

## 5. Define Sales acceptance

A Marketing handoff should not automatically become an opportunity.

Sales should review the account.

A simple status model could be:

### Accepted

Sales agrees that the account deserves follow-up.

### Nurture

The account fits, but timing or intent is not strong enough.

Return to Marketing nurture.

### Disqualified

The account does not meet agreed commercial criteria.

The reason should be captured.

This gives Marketing real feedback instead of silence.

---

## 6. Use a Service Level Agreement

Marketing and Sales should agree on expected behaviour.

A simple SLA could define:

- Which signals trigger a handoff
- Who owns the next step
- Expected response time
- How many follow-up attempts are required
- What happens if the account does not respond
- When the account returns to nurture
- Which rejection reasons must be recorded

For example:

**High-intent qualified account**

↓

**Sales notified**

↓

**Initial follow-up within agreed timeframe**

↓

**Accepted / nurture / disqualified**

↓

**Status captured in CRM**

The exact timeframe depends on the business.

The important part is that ownership is clear.

---

## 7. Feedback closes the loop

Sales feedback is one of the most valuable data sources for Demand Generation.

Marketing should regularly ask:

- Were the accounts relevant?
- Was the timing right?
- Were we reaching the right people?
- What objections appeared?
- Which messages resonated?
- Which leads became opportunities?
- Why were accounts rejected?

This improves:

- ICP
- Scoring
- Targeting
- Messaging
- Content
- Campaigns

The loop becomes:

**Marketing demand**

↓

**Sales conversation**

↓

**Commercial feedback**

↓

**Better Marketing**

---

## 8. Measure handoff quality

Useful metrics include:

### Marketing → Sales

- Qualified accounts
- Handoff volume
- Sales acceptance rate
- Time to first Sales action

### Sales progression

- Accepted → meeting
- Meeting → opportunity
- Opportunity value
- Pipeline created

### Quality

- Disqualification reasons
- No-response rate
- Lead quality by campaign
- Opportunity rate by segment

A high handoff volume with a low acceptance rate is usually not a success.

It may indicate:

- Weak targeting
- Weak qualification
- Poor scoring
- Misalignment between Marketing and Sales

---

## 9. Sales acceptance rate

One of the metrics I would watch closely is:

**Sales Accepted / Marketing Qualified**

For example:

If Marketing passes:

**100 qualified accounts**

and Sales accepts:

**70**

then:

**Sales acceptance rate = 70%**

That number creates a useful discussion.

If acceptance drops, investigate:

- Segment quality
- Campaign source
- Score threshold
- Buying intent
- Sales capacity
- Definition of qualification

The objective is not to chase 100%.

The objective is to make sure Marketing and Sales have a shared understanding of quality.

---

## 10. Avoid the MQL trap

The MQL can still be useful operationally.

But it should not become the main objective.

A Marketing team can hit an MQL target while pipeline stays flat.

That happens when:

**Lead volume increases**

but:

- Fit decreases
- Sales acceptance decreases
- Opportunity conversion falls
- Average opportunity value drops

The stronger hierarchy is:

**Qualified demand**

↓

**Sales accepted**

↓

**Opportunity**

↓

**Pipeline**

↓

**Revenue**

MQLs are a stage.

Not the business result.

---

## 11. Sales and Marketing should share pipeline

For a mature demand generation engine, pipeline is not only a Sales metric.

Marketing should understand:

- Pipeline created
- Pipeline influenced
- Opportunity rate
- Win rate
- Sales cycle
- Deal size
- Pipeline by segment
- Pipeline by campaign

Sales should understand:

- Which Marketing activity created demand
- Which content buyers consumed
- Which segments are responding
- Which campaigns are producing quality

This creates shared commercial accountability.

---

## 12. Example operating rhythm

A simple weekly Demand Gen / Sales review could cover:

### Pipeline

- New opportunities
- Pipeline created
- Pipeline movement

### Demand

- New qualified accounts
- High-intent accounts
- Priority target-account activity

### Handoff

- Accepted
- Rejected
- Awaiting follow-up

### Feedback

- Quality issues
- Objections
- Campaign insights
- Missing buying-group roles

### Decisions

- Which accounts need support?
- Which campaigns deserve more investment?
- Which segments are underperforming?
- What should Marketing change?

The meeting should end with decisions.

Not a dashboard tour.

---

The full system becomes:

**Market**

↓

**ICP**

↓

**Demand**

↓

**Engagement**

↓

**Qualification**

↓

**Sales handoff**

↓

**Opportunity**

↓

**Pipeline**

↓

**Revenue**

↓

**Sales feedback**

↓

**Better ICP, campaigns and scoring**

This is what makes the demand generation engine improve over time.

---

## Core principle

> **The handoff is not the end of Marketing's responsibility. It is the point where Marketing and Sales should become most commercially aligned.**
