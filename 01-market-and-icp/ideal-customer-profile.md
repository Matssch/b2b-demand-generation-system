# Ideal Customer Profile

## ICP is a commercial filter

An Ideal Customer Profile should not be a vague description of a company we would like to work with.

Its purpose is to answer:

> **Which companies are most likely to need the solution, successfully buy it and create meaningful commercial value?**

A strong ICP helps Marketing and Sales decide where to invest.

The basic logic is:

**Market opportunity → ICP → Priority accounts → Buying group → Demand → Pipeline**

---

## 1. Company fit

The first layer is firmographic fit.

Depending on the business, this could include:

- Company size
- Revenue
- Industry
- Geography
- Business model
- Growth stage
- Number of employees
- Technology environment
- Organisational complexity

These characteristics help narrow the market.

But firmographics alone do not create a strong ICP.

A company can look perfect on paper and still have no reason to buy.

---

## 2. Problem fit

The stronger question is:

> Does this company actually experience the problem we solve?

I would look for signals such as:

- Clear operational pain
- High cost of the current problem
- Existing inefficient processes
- Growth creating new complexity
- Regulation or market pressure
- Skills or capability gaps
- Increasing demand from customers
- Current solutions no longer scaling

This separates companies that simply match our database filters from companies that have a real commercial reason to change.

---

## 3. Value potential

Not every customer creates the same value.

The ICP should therefore consider the potential commercial relationship.

Relevant factors may include:

- Expected contract value
- Number of users or seats
- Expansion potential
- Cross-sell potential
- Retention potential
- Strategic importance
- Margin
- Lifetime value

This matters because customer value determines how much acquisition effort can be justified.

A €2K customer and a €200K customer should not automatically receive the same growth motion.

---

## 4. Ability to buy

Need does not automatically mean opportunity.

The account also needs the ability to act.

Buying capacity could include:

- Budget
- Decision-making authority
- Internal resources
- Procurement complexity
- Executive sponsorship
- Timing
- Existing priorities
- Ability to implement the solution

This helps distinguish:

**Good theoretical fit**

from:

**Realistic commercial opportunity**

---

## 5. Buying signals

Static ICP data tells us who could buy.

Behaviour tells us who may be moving closer to buying.

Useful signals could include:

- High-intent website visits
- Multiple visits from the same account
- Pricing or product-page engagement
- Webinar attendance
- Content downloads
- Demo requests
- Search behaviour
- Email engagement
- Event participation
- Product trials
- Sales conversations
- Third-party intent data

This creates an important distinction:

### Fit

Is this the right type of company?

### Intent

Is something happening now?

The strongest opportunities often sit where:

**High fit + High intent**

overlap.

---

## 6. Strategic fit

Some accounts may deserve attention beyond immediate revenue potential.

Examples include:

- Strong logo value
- Entry into a new market
- Strategic industry relevance
- Partnership potential
- Product-learning value
- Reference potential
- Expansion into a wider group

This should not override weak economics indefinitely.

But it can justify additional investment where the strategic benefit is clear.

---

## 7. Negative-fit criteria

A good ICP should also define who we do **not** want to pursue.

This prevents Marketing from creating volume that Sales cannot convert.

Negative-fit signals could include:

- Too small to create viable economics
- No relevant use case
- Geography we cannot serve
- Incompatible technology requirements
- Extremely low expected customer value
- Excessive implementation complexity
- No realistic budget
- High churn risk
- Poor regulatory fit
- Requirements the product cannot support

A strong demand generation engine needs exclusion criteria.

Otherwise every lead eventually becomes "worth following up."

That usually lasts until Sales opens the CRM.

---

## 8. ICP scoring

I would convert the ICP into a simple scoring model.

Example:

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Problem fit | 25% |
| Company fit | 20% |
| Value potential | 20% |
| Buying signals | 15% |
| Ability to buy | 10% |
| Strategic fit | 10% |

Each account could receive a score from 1–5 for each category.

For example:

| Account | Company fit | Problem fit | Value | Intent | Ability to buy | Strategic | Overall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Account A | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | High |
| Account B | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 3 | Medium |
| Account C | 3 | 2 | 2 | 5 | 3 | 2 | Low/Medium |

The exact weighting should change as real conversion data becomes available.

The model is a starting hypothesis, not permanent truth.

---

## 9. Fit and intent matrix

A useful way to translate scoring into action is:

| | Low Intent | High Intent |
|---|---|---|
| **High Fit** | Create demand | Prioritise now |
| **Low Fit** | Low priority | Qualify carefully |

### High fit + high intent

Strongest immediate commercial priority.

Marketing and Sales should coordinate quickly.

### High fit + low intent

Important future demand.

Use education, thought leadership, paid social, events and targeted nurture.

### Low fit + high intent

There may be demand, but qualification matters.

Do not automatically send every high-intent account to Sales.

### Low fit + low intent

Usually low priority.

Resources are better used elsewhere.

---

## 10. ICP should change the GTM motion

Once ICP quality is understood, the customer journey can change accordingly.

### Lower-value ICP

More automated motion:

**Paid / SEO → Landing page → Conversion → Automated lifecycle**

### Mid-value ICP

Hybrid motion:

**Demand generation → Engagement → Qualification → Sales**

### High-value ICP

More account-focused motion:

**Target account → Buying group → Account engagement → Sales coordination → Opportunity**

This prevents every customer from being forced into the same funnel.

---

## 11. ICP should influence channel strategy

The ICP also changes where Marketing invests.

For example:

### Smaller scalable accounts

Potential channels:

- Paid Search
- SEO
- Paid Social
- Lifecycle
- Product-led conversion

### Mid-market

Potential channels:

- Search
- LinkedIn
- Webinars
- Case studies
- Retargeting
- Lead nurture
- Sales outreach

### Strategic accounts

Potential channels:

- ABM
- Events
- Executive content
- Personalised campaigns
- Partnerships
- Account-level advertising
- Coordinated outbound

Channels follow the customer economics.

Not the other way around.

---

## 12. ICP should be validated with revenue data

The first ICP is a hypothesis.

Over time, I would compare it against actual commercial performance.

Questions include:

- Which segments create the most pipeline?
- Which companies convert fastest?
- Which accounts have the highest win rate?
- Which customers retain?
- Which accounts expand?
- Which characteristics correlate with poor conversion?
- Where is CAC highest?
- Which customers create the strongest lifetime value?

This creates a feedback loop:

**ICP hypothesis**

↓

**Marketing investment**

↓

**Pipeline**

↓

**Customers**

↓

**Retention & value**

↓

**ICP refinement**

The ICP should get better as the business learns.

---

## ICP output

A useful ICP should ultimately tell Marketing and Sales:

1. Which companies should we target?
2. Which problems matter most?
3. Which accounts have the highest value potential?
4. Which signals indicate buying intent?
5. Which accounts deserve Sales attention?
6. Which accounts should we deprioritise?
7. Which GTM motion fits the account?

If it cannot answer those questions, the ICP is probably still too theoretical.

---

## Core principle

> **An ICP is not a persona. It is a commercial prioritisation model.**

The objective is not to describe the perfect customer.

The objective is to decide **where Marketing and Sales have the highest probability of creating valuable pipeline.**
