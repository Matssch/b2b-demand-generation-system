# Demand Capture Engine
## Capture intent when the buyer is ready
Demand creation builds awareness and relevance. Demand capture focuses on the part of the market that is already showing stronger buying intent. The objective is not simply to generate more conversions. It is to make sure that existing demand moves into the right commercial journey. The model is:

**Intent → Relevant entry point → Conversion → Qualification → Sales / Self-service → Pipeline**

---

## 1. Identify high-intent behaviour
Not every interaction has the same commercial meaning. Examples of stronger intent can include:
- Product or solution searches
- Pricing-page visits
- Demo requests
- Contact requests
- Comparison searches
- Repeat visits
- Case-study engagement
- High-value content consumption
- Event attendance
- Multiple stakeholders from the same account engaging

The exact signals depend on the business. The important distinction is: **Engagement does not automatically equal buying intent.**

---

## 2. Search is a major demand capture channel
Paid Search and SEO are especially useful because the buyer is actively expressing a need. Search demand can be divided into different levels.

### Brand intent
The buyer already knows the company. Examples:
- Brand + product
- Brand + pricing
- Brand + reviews

These searches are usually high intent.

### Solution intent
The buyer knows what type of solution they need. Examples:
- B2B training platform
- AI workforce training
- Fleet management software
- Demand generation platform

These searches can create strong commercial opportunities.

### Problem intent
The buyer is searching around the problem. Examples:
- How to train employees in AI
- Reduce fleet operating costs
- Improve B2B conversion
- Increase sales pipeline

These searches may be earlier stage but still commercially relevant.

### Comparison intent
The buyer is actively evaluating options. Examples:
- Product A vs Product B
- Best platforms for...
- Alternative to...

These searches often sit close to a buying decision.

---

## 3. Match intent to the right landing page
Demand is wasted when the landing experience does not match the search. The principle is:

**Query → Intent → Message → Page → CTA**

A high-intent visitor should not land on a generic corporate homepage if a more relevant solution page exists.

Landing pages should answer:
1. Am I in the right place?
2. Does this solve my problem?
3. Is this relevant for a company like mine?
4. Why should I trust this solution?
5. What should I do next?

The closer the page matches the intent, the lower the friction.

---

## 4. Use different CTAs by buying stage
Not every visitor should be pushed toward the same conversion.

### Lower-friction CTA
Useful for earlier demand:
- Read guide
- View case study
- Watch webinar
- Explore solution

### Commercial CTA
Useful for stronger intent:
- Request demo
- Book consultation
- Contact Sales
- Start trial
- Request proposal

The CTA should match both the customer's intent and the commercial model.

---

## 5. Segment the conversion path
The same conversion should not trigger the same next step for every account.

### Smaller account
Possible journey:

**Search → Landing page → Sign-up → Automated onboarding**

### Mid-market account
Possible journey:

**Search → Landing page → Lead → Qualification → Sales**

### Strategic account
Possible journey:

**Intent signal → Account identified → Buying-group review → Sales coordination**

This is where demand capture connects back to segmentation.

---

## 6. Qualification starts immediately
A form submission is not automatically qualified demand. I would combine several signals.

### Fit
Does the account match the ICP?

### Intent
How strong is the buying behaviour?

### Engagement
How much interaction has happened?

### Value
What is the potential commercial opportunity? The strongest commercial signals usually sit where: **High fit + High intent + Meaningful engagement** overlap.

---

## 7. Reduce unnecessary friction
Demand capture also means removing barriers. Potential friction can include:
- Generic landing pages
- Long forms
- Weak mobile experience
- Slow website performance
- Unclear positioning
- Too many CTAs
- Poor proof
- Complicated sign-up
- Unclear next steps
- Slow Sales follow-up

A company can invest heavily in demand creation and still lose the opportunity at the final stage. This is why CRO is part of demand generation.

---

## 8. Retarget existing demand
Not every interested buyer converts immediately. Retargeting can help continue the journey. Useful audiences might include:

- High-intent page visitors
- Pricing-page visitors
- Case-study readers
- Webinar attendees
- Abandoned demo visitors
- Known target accounts
- Existing leads

The message should reflect what the buyer already knows. Someone who has visited pricing does not need the same message as someone seeing the company for the first time.

---

## 9. Connect digital intent with Sales
For higher-value accounts, digital behaviour should help Sales prioritise. Example:

**High ICP fit** → **Repeated solution-page visits** → **Multiple contacts from the account** → **Case-study engagement** → **Potential Sales priority**

This does not mean every website signal should trigger an SDR call. It means Marketing should surface meaningful commercial behaviour.

---

## 10. Speed matters
High intent can decay quickly. If someone:
- Requests a demo
- Asks for pricing
- Completes a high-intent form
- Shows clear account-level buying behaviour

the follow-up process should be clear. Marketing and Sales should agree on:
- What qualifies as a priority signal
- Who owns the follow-up
- Expected response time
- What information Sales receives
- What happens if Sales does not accept the lead

Demand capture is partly a process problem.

---

## 11. Measure demand capture through the funnel
I would monitor:

### Acquisition
- High-intent traffic
- Cost per click
- Landing-page traffic

### Conversion
- Conversion rate
- Cost per conversion
- Demo / contact rate

### Qualification
- Qualified demand
- Sales acceptance
- Cost per qualified opportunity

### Pipeline
- Opportunities
- Pipeline created
- Pipeline value
- Opportunity conversion
- Win rate

This creates a progression from: **Traffic** toward: **Commercial outcome**

---

## 12. Search performance should connect to pipeline
A keyword with a cheap CPC is not automatically valuable. A more useful question is: Which searches ultimately create qualified commercial opportunities? Over time, I would connect:

**Keyword / campaign**  → **Landing page** → **Lead / account** → **Qualification** → **Opportunity** → **Pipeline**

This allows channel optimisation to move beyond surface-level efficiency.

---

## 13. Demand capture and demand creation should reinforce each other
These are not separate systems. Demand creation may lead to:
- More branded searches
- Higher direct traffic
- More target-account engagement
- Better response to Paid Search
- Higher conversion rates

Demand capture then reveals which problems and segments are showing stronger buying intent. That information can improve future demand creation.

The loop becomes:

**Create demand** → **Capture intent** → **Observe what converts** → **Improve targeting and messaging** → **Create better demand**

---

## Core principle
**Demand capture is not about collecting every possible lead. It is about converting real buying intent into the right commercial journey.**

The objective is:

**Right account → Right intent → Right experience → Right Sales action → Pipeline**
